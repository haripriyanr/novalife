## 2026-09-15 — Vivado synthesis (real numbers, failing design)

Path synthesised: PS DDR -> AXI DMA MM2S -> AES-128-CTR -> LSB embed -> DMA S2MM -> DDR.
This was the v1 vault-encryption path, **not** the tag pipeline.

| Resource | Used | Available | Utilisation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Slice LUTs | 13,905 | 17,600 | 79.0% |
| Slice registers | 4,418 | 35,200 | 12.6% |
| Slices | 3,881 | 4,400 | 88.2% |
| BRAM (RAMB36E1) | 2 | 40 | 5.0% |
| DSP48 | 0 | 80 | 0% |

**Timing: WNS = -38.641 ns, TNS = -4494.661 ns, 122 failing endpoints at 100 MHz.**
Critical path: `aes128_ctr_axis` key schedule to output, 43 logic levels, 48.4 ns of delay
against a 10 ns period. The design does not close timing.

Consequences to remember before designing the PL cores:
1. The AES core needs pipelining or a lower clock; 43 combinational levels will not do.
2. Adding a DCT, a QIM quantiser and an adaptive-delta engine was estimated at another
   6,000-8,000 LUTs. **That does not fit in the xc7z010 at 79% LUT / 88% slice.** The tag
   cores have to be small (polar encode, mask, tag MAC) or the part is wrong.
3. Budget for the project stays `<75%` LUT, `<40%` DSP, `<40%` BRAM; the AES path already
   broke it.

## One correction to our own history

Commit `e551157` says "complete Vivado hardware synthesis and program bitstream onto physical
board". The implementation report for that design shows **0 LUTs and 0 registers used** — the
bitstream that was programmed was the placeholder VexRiscv wrapper, not a working design. On
2026-09-17 the JTAG probe again reported the device as not programmed. Treat that commit as a
toolchain exercise, not a working system.
